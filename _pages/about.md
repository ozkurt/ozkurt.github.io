I am fascinated by microbiomes across diverse hosts, and my research asks how microbial communities assemble, persist and are passed on, from wheat seeds to the human gut across the life course.

I currently work in the Human Studies Team at the [Quadram Institute](https://quadram.ac.uk/people/ezgi-ozkurt/) in Norwich, UK, where I led the **PEARL-AGE** family cohort study on the transmission of gut microbes across generations. My work combines wet-lab method development, such as enriching the rare microbial biosphere in faecal samples, with large-scale computational analysis of public gut metagenomes to identify age-related bacterial guilds. I also develop open tools for microbiome data analysis, including [LotuS2](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40168-022-01365-1) for amplicon sequencing.

I did my PhD in the Environmental Genomics group at the Max Planck Institute for Evolutionary Biology (Kiel University, 2020), where I studied how domestication shaped the wheat metaorganism. Before that, I studied Molecular Biology & Genetics (BSc) and Biology (MSc) at Middle East Technical University, Ankara.

I will soon join **Boğaziçi University**, Institute of Environmental Sciences, as an Assistant Professor, supported by a TÜBİTAK 2232-A fellowship.

**Interests:** microbial ecology · community assembly · vertical transmission · seed microbiomes · domestication and the microbiome · synthetic communities (SynComs) · numerical ecology · microbiome methods & teaching

For a full list of my publications, see my [Google Scholar profile](https://scholar.google.com/citations?user=VyalptsAAAAJ). You can reach me at [ezgi.ozkurt@quadram.ac.uk](mailto:ezgi.ozkurt@quadram.ac.uk).
